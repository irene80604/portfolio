Executive summary

AI is most valuable not when it produces the answer, but when it makes the reasoning easier to explore, challenge, and document.

As qualitative research scales, designers face a dilemma: spend weeks manually coding transcripts, or use naive AI prompts that hallucinate insights and strip out human context. To solve this, I designed a multi-stage AI research pipeline that acts as a thinking partner. By enforcing strict evidence-tracing protocols, this workflow drastically accelerates qualitative synthesis while ensuring human judgment remains in full control of product decisions.

Operational Challenge

The difficult part isn't collecting the data, but interpreting it.

When designers ask AI for quick insights directly from raw transcripts, they risk importing unverified assumptions and hallucinated summaries directly onto their product roadmaps.

Failure of Naive AI

Jumping directly from raw transcripts to "give me key insights" creates summaries that cannot be traced back to real user statements. Unverified assumptions disguise themselves as findings, creating risk for product roadmaps.

Human bottleneck

Manual thematic analysis remains the gold standard for accuracy, but its time-intensive nature often leads teams to skip qualitative synthesis during fast-paced continuous discovery sprints.

8-Stage Research Pipeline

Design Philosophy — AI as a thinking partner

AI is most valuable not when it produces the final answer, but when it makes reasoning easier to explore, challenge, and document. The researcher remains responsible for human judgment, sign-offs, and final interpretation. To preserve evidence tracing, every stage in this modular pipeline uses isolated prompts with a single task.

“Separate what participants said from what we think it means, and never let an assumption travel disguised as a finding.”
1

Data cleaning

Clean and structure the transcript while preserving hesitations, repetitions, and contradictions as signals.

AI prompt could be...

Clean this transcript and separate speakers. Do NOT remove hesitations or self-corrections — tag them like [hesitation]. Return a change log.

2

Behavioral coding

Code four parallel dimensions per statement, behaviour, context/trigger, emotional register, certainty, and keep every label neutral and descriptive.

AI prompt could be...

For each statement return: quote | neutral behaviour | context | emotion | certainty | self-reported/observed. Describe the action, don't infer motivation.

3

Pattern identification across participants

Com-B model · Snapshots

Decide a significance formula before looking into it (frequency × severity × uniqueness × relevance).

AI prompt could be...

Show code in 3+ participants, direct contradictions, and single-occurrence code. Do not rank by frequency.

4

Thematic clustering

Thematic Analysis · MECE (only for communication)

Let themes emerge through Thematic Analysis, then organize the findings into an opportunity tree.

AI prompt could be...

Group these patterns into themes in 3 different ways. Name themes using language from the data, and note overlaps and misfits.

5

JTBD derivation

JTBD framework

Derive jobs from themes with functional, emotional and social dimensions, and map the forces of progress — push, pull, anxiety, habit. Anchor each job to a quote.

AI prompt could be...

Turn each theme into a JTBD with functional/emotional/social dimensions. Critique each: is it a job, or a feature/solution? Rewrite weak ones and cite the quote.

6

Competing explanation testing

Occam's razor principle

From each theme/JTBD, force at least one alternative reading and state why you reject or retain it.

AI prompt could be...

Give 2-3 alternative explanations of the same evidence, and state what additional data would distinguish between them.

7

Insight formulation

Write each insight as pattern + meaning + implication. Every concept must trace to a specific quote.

AI prompt could be...

Draft an insight from this pattern. Then highlight every claim NOT directly supported by a quote and flag it as an assumption.

8

Opportunity framing

Pyramid + MECE (only for presentation)

Build the chain from Insight, UX problem, Opportunity to Recommendation. Flag any inferred link.

AI prompt could be...

Restructure my final recommendations to be as MECE as possible for a stakeholder deck. Flag overlap and gaps, but do not invent recommendations to fill gaps.

Analytical Example

Evidence vs. Assumption —
It's about uncertainty, rather than speed

Demonstrate how single-word additions by AI can distort research findings if left unchallenged.

Weak interpretation
“When I'm stuck, I want to generate directions so I can get started quickly.”

Flaw: The word "quickly" adds an assumption about speed that the participant never actually mentioned. It was about feeling stuck because of uncertainty, not gaining speed.

Strong interpretation
“When I'm faced with an unclear brief and don't know where to start, I want to reduce uncertainty about the problem space so I can choose a direction with confidence.”

Rigor: Every word is earned from the evidence. The job is about resolving uncertainty, not gaining speed. Confidence came from the data — "quickly" did not.

Reasoning

COM-B model

Analytical

Presentation

Pyramid + MECE

MECE organizes clear, non-overlapping recommendations, while the Pyramid Principle makes sure the story leads with the main conclusion.

Good habits to make AI-assisted analysis more traceable

01

Never let AI generate and validate in the same chat

Draft in one prompt. Attack the draft in a separate one. Generation and critique are different cognitive modes.

02

Keep a disagreement log

It prevents the system from hiding uncertainty by recording what the AI concluded, what another AI, analyst, or reviewer concluded differently, why they disagreed, and what evidence each side used.

03

Always keep quotes next to the insight

No quote means it's a hypothesis, rather than a finding.
